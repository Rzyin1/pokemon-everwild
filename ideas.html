Approved concept

Starter selection system

  • 18 original starter lines: one for each Pokémon type.
  • Each starter line has three evolutionary stages, for 54 starter Pokémon total.
  • At the professor's lab, three different-type starters are randomly offered.
  • The player examines the three choices and picks one.
To decide

Rival selection, reroll rules, and whether other starters become obtainable later.

Approved

Pokédex and Pokémon design

  • Regional Pokédex size stays open-ended.
  • Design one original Pokémon at a time.
  • Pokémon in the same evolution line normally share their level-up moves and learn levels, unless we approve an exception.
  • Current completed line: #001 Wolvyn → #002 Wolvigor → #003 Wolvage (Normal).
  • Balance can be revised during development and playtesting.
Future development

Game systems and world

We'll add ideas for the region, story, characters, exploration, battles, encounters, progression, and other gameplay systems as we discuss them. Nothing is locked in here yet.

Gameplay proposals

League rules and gym progression

Idea #1 — League entry age

Proposed

Trainers are 18 years old when they enter the Pokémon League.

Idea #2 — Pokémon registration

Proposed

Players must register their Pokémon through the League before using them in battle.

To decide: All battles, or only official League-sanctioned battles?

Idea #3 — Open gym challenge

Proposed

Players can challenge gyms in any order. Each Gym Leader adjusts their team based on the player's progression.

  • Option A: Scale by the player's Pokémon levels.
  • Option B: Scale by accumulated League points.
  • Option C: A hybrid of both, if later approved.

To decide: Which progression measure determines the Gym Leader's team?

League progression · Expanded concept

League points, catching zones, and training

Direction established

Progression should reward competitive development, not simply allow players to overlevel their team and overpower every challenge.

League points

  • Players earn League points by defeating trainers and Gym Leaders.
  • League points are distinct from Pokémon experience levels.
  • Gym progression should account for League standing; exact scaling rules remain to be determined.

Designated Pokémon-catching zones

  • Wild Pokémon are caught in designated areas, including safari-style zones, caves, and other habitats.
  • Players pay an entry fee to access these areas.
  • Newly caught Pokémon can be registered at the zone exit.
  • Wild Pokémon should generally be unevolved (first-stage species). Whether evolved wild Pokémon ever appear is not yet finalized.

Registration and training

  • Unregistered Pokémon may battle wild Pokémon.
  • Pokémon must be registered before participating in official League battles.
  • Players are expected to train and evolve Pokémon rather than catch already powerful evolved teams.
  • Additional training methods will be designed for bringing newly caught Pokémon up to speed late in the game.
To decide

How League points are awarded and protected from farming; gym team scaling; entry fees; whether any evolved wild Pokémon appear; and alternative training methods.

Progression concept · Under discussion

League Points in battles and training-based leveling

Clarified

League Points are always involved in trainer battles, including Gym Leader battles. Trainer battles have a competitive progression purpose even if they award no Pokémon experience.

Proposed

Replace battle-earned experience with Pokémon training as the method for gaining levels, learning level-up moves, and reaching evolution levels. Keep trainer progression (League Points) separate from Pokémon development (training).

To decide: Whether all trainer battles award points or whether losses can affect points; how points scale by opponent standing; how training sessions work; and how late-game Pokémon catch up.

League battles · Approved rule

Defeat costs League Points, not a whiteout

Approved

When a player loses a trainer battle, the player loses League Points rather than experiencing the traditional Pokémon whiteout/blackout penalty. Winning trainer battles earns League Points. Every League battle therefore has competitive stakes.

To decide

Exact point gains and losses, whether League Points can go below zero, how fainted Pokémon are handled, and what happens immediately after a defeat. No example point amounts are finalized.

Economy · Approved concept

Pokémon Centers charge fees

Approved

Pokémon Centers are paid services rather than free healing locations. This makes money management part of the adventure alongside paid entry to designated Pokémon-catching zones.

To decide

Healing prices, whether fees depend on the number or condition of Pokémon, and which additional Center services may cost money. No specific prices are approved yet.

League economy and rankings · New direction

Trainer battles pay money and change League rating

Confirmed

Defeating trainers rewards both money and League Points. Trainer battles are a primary source of competitive progress and income; losses reduce League Points rather than causing a traditional whiteout.

Proposed: Elo-style rating

League Points may use an Elo-like rating system: beating a higher-rated trainer earns more points than beating a lower-rated trainer, and losing to a lower-rated trainer costs more points than losing to a higher-rated one. Opponent strength would be measured by League rating rather than Pokémon level alone.

Illustrative example only: with K=32 and a player rating of 1000, beating an equally rated opponent earns about 16 rating points, while losing costs about 16. No starting rating, K-factor, ranking tiers, anti-farming rules, or money payout formula has been approved yet.

League rating · Under discussion

SPR-inspired 50-point starting rating

Proposed

Inspired by Scoreholio SPR, new League entrants would begin at a rating of 50.00 and move up or down based on trainer-battle results. This is an inspiration for the rating scale, not a claim to use Scoreholio's proprietary rating formula.

Open design challenge: Assign fair initial ratings to NPC trainers. Proposed approach: rate NPCs according to intended competitive strength, considering team composition, battle AI, movesets, and trainer experience rather than Pokémon levels alone. Rating bands, exact formula, rating floor/ceiling, and whether NPC ratings change dynamically remain undecided.

Progression redesign · Proposed

Three distinct battle activities

New preferred direction — not yet finalized

Separate battle rewards by location and purpose instead of awarding League Points for every trainer encounter.

  • Regular trainer battles: Award money, not League Points. Proposed to award no Pokémon EXP.
  • Official League battle buildings: Battle Tower-style facilities award or deduct League Points/rating through ranked battles.
  • Pokémon training facilities: Roguelike-style battle runs start against weak Pokémon and become progressively more difficult. Pokémon gain EXP by defeating opponents inside these facilities, allowing leveling, moves, and evolution.

This proposal revises the earlier idea that all trainer battles change League rating and the earlier training-only, no-battle-EXP concept. Those earlier entries remain as design history, not current preferred mechanics.

To decide

Whether gyms also affect League rating, training-run floor structure and rewards, what happens when a run ends, whether regular battles award EXP, and how to prevent overleveling in ranked competition.

Gym difficulty · Decision pending

Preventing overleveling: scaling or level caps

Two options under consideration

Option A — Open-order gyms with scaling: Players challenge gyms in any order; Gym Leader teams scale to the challenger's selected battle team. The exact scaling calculation remains undecided.

Option B — Ordered gyms with level caps: Gyms follow a sequence (Gym 1, Gym 2, Gym 3, etc.) with fixed difficulty and a level cap at each stage. The cap values and handling of overleveled Pokémon are undecided.

Neither approach has been selected. The purpose of either is to prevent players from trivializing gym battles through training-facility overleveling.
