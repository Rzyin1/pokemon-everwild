Approved concept

Starter selection system

  • 18 original starter lines: one for each Pokémon type.
  • Each starter line has three evolutionary stages, for 54 starter Pokémon total.
  • At the professor's lab, three different-type starters are randomly offered.
  • The player examines the three choices and picks one.
To decide

Rival selection, reroll rules, and whether other starters become obtainable later.

Approved

Pokédex and Pokémon design

  • Regional Pokédex size stays open-ended.
  • Design one original Pokémon at a time.
  • Pokémon in the same evolution line normally share their level-up moves and learn levels, unless we approve an exception.
  • Current completed line: #001 Wolvyn → #002 Wolvigor → #003 Wolvage (Normal).
  • Balance can be revised during development and playtesting.

Game systems and world

We'll add ideas for the region, story, characters, exploration, battles, encounters, progression, and other gameplay systems as we discuss them. Nothing is locked in here yet.

Gameplay proposals

League rules and gym progression

Idea #1 — League entry age

Proposed

Trainers are 18 years old when they enter the Pokémon League.

Idea #2 — Pokémon registration

Proposed

Players must register their Pokémon through the League before using them in battle.

To decide: All battles, or only official League-sanctioned battles?

Idea #3 — Open gym challenge

Proposed

Players can challenge gyms in any order. Each Gym Leader adjusts their team based on the player's progression.

  • Option A: Scale by the player's Pokémon levels.
  • Option B: Scale by accumulated League points.
  • Option C: A hybrid of both, if later approved.

To decide: Which progression measure determines the Gym Leader's team?

League progression · Expanded concept

League points, catching zones, and training

Direction established

Progression should reward competitive development, not simply allow players to overlevel their team and overpower every challenge.

League points

  • Players earn League points by defeating trainers and Gym Leaders.
  • League points are distinct from Pokémon experience levels.
  • Gym progression should account for League standing; exact scaling rules remain to be determined.

Designated Pokémon-catching zones

  • Wild Pokémon are caught in designated areas, including safari-style zones, caves, and other habitats.
  • Players pay an entry fee to access these areas.
  • Newly caught Pokémon can be registered at the zone exit.
  • Wild Pokémon should generally be unevolved (first-stage species). Whether evolved wild Pokémon ever appear is not yet finalized.

Registration and training

  • Unregistered Pokémon may battle wild Pokémon.
  • Pokémon must be registered before participating in official League battles.
  • Players are expected to train and evolve Pokémon rather than catch already powerful evolved teams.
  • Additional training methods will be designed for bringing newly caught Pokémon up to speed late in the game.
To decide

How League points are awarded and protected from farming; gym team scaling; entry fees; whether any evolved wild Pokémon appear; and alternative training methods.

Progression concept · Under discussion

League Points in battles and training-based leveling

Clarified

League Points are always involved in trainer battles, including Gym Leader battles. Trainer battles have a competitive progression purpose even if they award no Pokémon experience.

Proposed

Replace battle-earned experience with Pokémon training as the method for gaining levels, learning level-up moves, and reaching evolution levels. Keep trainer progression (League Points) separate from Pokémon development (training).

To decide: Whether all trainer battles award points or whether losses can affect points; how points scale by opponent standing; how training sessions work; and how late-game Pokémon catch up.

League battles · Approved rule

Defeat costs League Points, not a whiteout

Approved

When a player loses a trainer battle, the player loses League Points rather than experiencing the traditional Pokémon whiteout/blackout penalty. Winning trainer battles earns League Points. Every League battle therefore has competitive stakes.

To decide

Exact point gains and losses, whether League Points can go below zero, how fainted Pokémon are handled, and what happens immediately after a defeat. No example point amounts are finalized.

Economy · Approved concept

Pokémon Centers charge fees

Approved

Pokémon Centers are paid services rather than free healing locations. This makes money management part of the adventure alongside paid entry to designated Pokémon-catching zones.

To decide

Healing prices, whether fees depend on the number or condition of Pokémon, and which additional Center services may cost money. No specific prices are approved yet.

League economy and rankings · New direction

Trainer battles pay money and change League rating

Confirmed

Defeating trainers rewards both money and League Points. Trainer battles are a primary source of competitive progress and income; losses reduce League Points rather than causing a traditional whiteout.

Proposed: Elo-style rating

League Points may use an Elo-like rating system: beating a higher-rated trainer earns more points than beating a lower-rated trainer, and losing to a lower-rated trainer costs more points than losing to a higher-rated one. Opponent strength would be measured by League rating rather than Pokémon level alone.

Illustrative example only: with K=32 and a player rating of 1000, beating an equally rated opponent earns about 16 rating points, while losing costs about 16. No starting rating, K-factor, ranking tiers, anti-farming rules, or money payout formula has been approved yet.

League rating · Under discussion

SPR-inspired 50-point starting rating

Proposed

Inspired by Scoreholio SPR, new League entrants would begin at a rating of 50.00 and move up or down based on trainer-battle results. This is an inspiration for the rating scale, not a claim to use Scoreholio's proprietary rating formula.

Open design challenge: Assign fair initial ratings to NPC trainers. Proposed approach: rate NPCs according to intended competitive strength, considering team composition, battle AI, movesets, and trainer experience rather than Pokémon levels alone. Rating bands, exact formula, rating floor/ceiling, and whether NPC ratings change dynamically remain undecided.

Progression redesign · Proposed

Three distinct battle activities

New preferred direction — not yet finalized

Separate battle rewards by location and purpose instead of awarding League Points for every trainer encounter.

  • Regular trainer battles: Award money, not League Points. Proposed to award no Pokémon EXP.
  • Official League battle buildings: Battle Tower-style facilities award or deduct League Points/rating through ranked battles.
  • Pokémon training facilities: Roguelike-style battle runs start against weak Pokémon and become progressively more difficult. Pokémon gain EXP by defeating opponents inside these facilities, allowing leveling, moves, and evolution.

This proposal revises the earlier idea that all trainer battles change League rating and the earlier training-only, no-battle-EXP concept. Those earlier entries remain as design history, not current preferred mechanics.

To decide

Whether gyms also affect League rating, training-run floor structure and rewards, what happens when a run ends, whether regular battles award EXP, and how to prevent overleveling in ranked competition.

Gym difficulty · Decision pending

Preventing overleveling: scaling or level caps

Two options under consideration

Option A — Open-order gyms with scaling: Players challenge gyms in any order; Gym Leader teams scale to the challenger's selected battle team. The exact scaling calculation remains undecided.

Option B — Ordered gyms with level caps: Gyms follow a sequence (Gym 1, Gym 2, Gym 3, etc.) with fixed difficulty and a level cap at each stage. The cap values and handling of overleveled Pokémon are undecided.

Neither approach has been selected. The purpose of either is to prevent players from trivializing gym battles through training-facility overleveling.

World and gyms · Approved direction

Open exploration, fixed-order gyms

Approved

Players may freely explore the region and visit catching zones before each gym, allowing them to seek out the Pokémon and Pokémon types they want for their team. Gym Leaders are challenged in a fixed order (Gym 1, Gym 2, Gym 3, etc.) with level caps to control difficulty, rather than scaling every gym to the player's team.

To decide

Exact level caps, gym sequence, geographic access restrictions if any, and how overleveled Pokémon are handled. Suggested design principle—not yet approved: make at least one Pokémon of every type obtainable before Gym 1.

Transportation · Proposed

Paid railway fast travel

Proposed

A train network allows players to spend money on fast travel between major parts of the open region, including predetermined gym locations and areas near catching zones. This prevents long return walks while letting players explore widely and assemble their preferred team before each gym.

Potential implementation: stations in major cities and habitat hubs, selectable destinations, short travel transitions, and ticket fares that vary by distance.

To decide

Whether all stations are available from the start or must first be visited, how much tickets cost, exact station coverage, and whether certain destinations have progression restrictions. Recommendation: provide broad early access to support catching Pokémon before Gym 1.

Transportation · Approved

Train network open from the start

Approved

All train stations and routes are available immediately. Players do not have to discover stations, earn badges, or unlock train destinations. They can buy a ticket to any station from the beginning of the game, provided they can afford the fare. The railway supports free exploration and catching preferred Pokémon before fixed-order gym challenges.

To decide

Exact ticket pricing and station locations.

Trainer economy · Approved

Regular trainer rematches: two-hour cooldown

Approved

Each regular trainer can be battled again once every two hours. Rematches provide another opportunity to earn money. The region should include enough trainers that players can reliably fund train fares, Pokémon Center fees, and catching-zone entry without relying on immediate repeat battles.

To decide

Whether the two-hour timer uses real-world elapsed time (including while the game is closed) or active playtime; exact trainer payouts and overall trainer density. Recommended: individual real-time cooldowns per trainer.

Trainer rematches · Implementation feasibility

Real-time cooldown is technically feasible

Implementation preference, pending testing

For a standalone PC game, save each trainer's last-defeated timestamp and compare it with the computer's current time. This allows a two-hour rematch cooldown to elapse even while the game is closed. Local system clock changes could bypass it; a server-authoritative clock would be more resistant but is unnecessary for an initial single-player version.

Final choice between real-world elapsed time and active gameplay time remains conditional on implementation and testing.

Economy · Approved

Regenerating quest boards provide repeatable income

Approved

Quest boards around the region offer jobs that players can complete for money. Available quests regenerate, giving players a repeatable way to earn money beyond regular trainer battles and their two-hour rematch cooldowns. Quest-board income helps fund Pokémon Center fees, train tickets, and catching-zone entry.

To decide

Quest types, regeneration interval, how many quests can be active at once, and reward amounts. Possible quest types include deliveries, gathering supplies, and helping researchers; these examples are not yet approved.

Quest boards · Approved

Immediate quest replacement

Approved

When a player completes a quest and claims its reward, the quest board immediately generates a replacement quest. There is no daily reset or cooldown on quest-board replenishment, so quests remain a reliable repeatable source of money.

To decide

Quest variety, reward amounts, and how many quests are available simultaneously.

Gym progression · Approved

Pokémon stop earning EXP at the current level cap

Approved

Pokémon earn experience normally until reaching the level cap for the player's current gym stage. At the cap, they stop gaining EXP. Defeating the required gym advances the cap and permits further leveling. This prevents overleveling rather than scaling Pokémon down for gym battles.

To decide

Exact caps for each gym and whether EXP earned while capped is discarded or stored. Recommendation: discard it to prevent instant multi-level gains when the cap increases.

Gym level caps · Approved

EXP above the cap is discarded

Approved

Once a Pokémon reaches the current gym-stage level cap, any additional EXP it would earn is permanently discarded, not stored or banked. After the next gym is defeated and the cap increases, the Pokémon resumes earning EXP normally from its existing progress.

Core progression · Approved direction

Gyms and League form one progression system

Approved

Gym challenges are part of the Pokémon League rather than a separate campaign. Gym progression and League rating belong to the same overall competitive structure; the precise relationship between gym victories, rating, and eligibility is still to be designed. Defeating the next gym advances the Pokémon level cap.

The game is not committed to exactly eight gyms; the number of gyms remains open. A separate storyline may be added later, but the current design focus is the interconnected gym and League system.

To decide

How League rating interacts with gym eligibility and results, the total number of gyms, and the League championship qualification rules.

League gym eligibility · Approved

League Points required to challenge each gym

Approved

Players must earn enough League Points through official League battles to qualify to challenge the next Gym Leader. Gyms and League progression are one connected system: regular trainer battles provide money, training facilities provide Pokémon EXP up to the current cap, and League battle facilities provide the points needed to access the next gym. Defeating that gym advances the level cap.

To decide

Exact League Point thresholds for each gym, whether eligibility checks current rating or highest achieved rating, and whether points are spent on entry. Recommendation: use a rating threshold without deducting points for entering.

Defeat consequences · New decision

Gym qualification stays unlocked; losses may cost money

Approved

Once a player has earned enough League Points to qualify for a gym, that eligibility is permanent even if their current rating later drops below the threshold. The player does not need to requalify for that same gym.

Proposed penalty

Battle defeats should have a money penalty so players lose something meaningful. In official League battles, rating may still decrease, but losing rating does not revoke previously earned gym eligibility. The exact money-loss formula, whether it applies to every battle type, and the handling of insufficient funds remain undecided. A percentage of carried money is one possible approach, not yet approved.

Gym eligibility · Approved clarification

Current League rating determines gym eligibility

Approved

Qualification for the next gym uses the player's current League rating, not their peak or previously achieved rating. If the player reaches a gym's required rating but subsequently loses League Points and drops below the threshold before challenging the gym, they become ineligible and must earn those points back.

Under consideration

Whether losing a battle should also cost money, and in which battle types, has not been decided.

Regular trainer battles · Approved

Win money or lose money

Approved

Regular trainer battles use money as their stake. If the player wins, they earn money from the opposing trainer. If the player loses, they lose money. These battles do not award or deduct League Points; official League battles handle League rating changes.

To decide

Exact payouts and defeat penalties, whether they vary by trainer difficulty, and what happens when a player cannot afford the full loss amount.

Regular trainer stakes · Approved

Equal money won and lost

Approved

Each regular trainer battle has a defined money stake. Winning earns exactly that amount; losing deducts exactly the same amount. Regular trainer battles do not affect League Points. For illustration, a $500-stake trainer awards $500 on a win and costs $500 on a loss; this is not a finalized payout amount.

To decide

Trainer-specific stake amounts and what happens if the player has less money than the stake.

League matchmaking · Approved

Automatically assigned League opponents

Approved

Official League battle facilities automatically assign the player's next opponent based on their current League rating. Players cannot select opponents to farm easy League Points. Matchmaking should generally seek opponents of comparable competitive strength.

To decide

Exact allowable rating range, how to handle limited NPC opponent pools, repeat-opponent restrictions, and whether occasional higher-rated challenges are offered.

League battles · Preferred design

Scale League opponents instead of imposing a separate level cap

Approved direction

Official League battles use automatic Elo-style rating matchmaking and opponent scaling rather than an additional fixed Pokémon level cap. The existing gym-stage level cap still controls the maximum level players can train to. Proposed implementation: scale opponent Pokémon levels to the player's registered battle team while using League rating to select opponent skill, AI, movesets, and team composition.

To decide

Exact level-scaling formula, whether scaling is based on highest or average team level, and how to balance opponents with very different team compositions.

League opponent scaling · Approved

Scale to the highest-level Pokémon

Approved

For official League battles, the opponent's Pokémon levels scale using the highest-level Pokémon on the player's entered team, not the team's average level. For example, a team whose highest-level Pokémon is Level 30 faces an opponent team scaled around Level 30. League rating separately controls matchmaking and opponent competitive strength.

To decide

Whether every opponent Pokémon matches that level exactly or individual team members vary around it, and how roster locking works when entering a League battle.

League team size · Under discussion

Six-Pokémon teams are accessible early

Design premise

Players can travel widely and catch the Pokémon they want before gym challenges, so assembling a full team of six should be practical early in the game. Proposed League format: standard six-on-six battles at all ratings, with higher-rated opponents differentiated by strategy, movesets, switching, and team synergy rather than by having more Pokémon.

To decide

Whether all official League battles require six Pokémon or permit smaller entered teams, and whether every Pokémon species is available immediately or becomes accessible through exploration.

Battle Frontier · Proposed

Handcrafted Frontier trainers and teams

Under consideration

Instead of procedurally generating League opponent teams, manually author each Battle Frontier trainer, including identity, assigned League rating, Pokémon species, abilities, moves, held items, and strategy. The facility automatically selects an appropriate trainer based on the player's rating. A trainer's handcrafted roster stays consistent across encounters, while Pokémon levels could still scale to the highest-level Pokémon on the player's entered team.

Tradeoff: more control over quality and balance, but more authoring effort as the roster grows. Not yet finalized whether this replaces the League battle facilities or is their official format.

Battle Frontier · Approved

Handcrafted trainers with dynamic Pokémon levels

Approved

Frontier trainers are manually authored, including their Pokémon teams and strategies, but their Pokémon levels scale to the highest-level Pokémon the player brings. Players revisit the League facilities throughout gym progression, so the same Frontier trainers must remain viable against teams spanning low levels through Level 100. Opponent assignment continues to use League rating.

To decide

Whether manually assigned movesets stay fixed at all scaled levels or are adjusted for each level range; how to handle moves or evolutions that normally require higher levels.

Battle Frontier matchmaking · Approved direction

Generated early teams, handcrafted high-rating teams

Approved direction

At lower League ratings, Battle Frontier opponents use randomly generated teams, with Pokémon levels scaled to the player's highest-level Pokémon. After a higher League-rating threshold, opponents instead use deliberately handcrafted teams with coordinated Pokémon, moves, abilities, and competitive synergy. Automatic opponent assignment by League rating remains in effect.

To decide

The rating threshold for switching from generated teams to handcrafted teams will be set after determining gym League-rating requirements. Generation constraints, movesets, and exact competitive-team designs are also undecided.

Battle Frontier generation · Approved

Generated Pokémon evolve according to their level

Approved

Randomly generated lower-rating Battle Frontier teams use the evolutionary stage appropriate to each Pokémon's generated level. The generator selects an evolutionary line, scales the Pokémon's level to the League opponent's battle level, then resolves its species based on that line's evolution requirements. For example, Wolvyn is used at levels 1–16, Wolvigor at 17–34, and Wolvage at 35–100.

To decide

How to handle non-level evolution methods, such as stones, friendship, trading, or special conditions, when generating teams.
